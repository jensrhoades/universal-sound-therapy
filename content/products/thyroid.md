## About Thyroid

The thyroid gland is responsible for producing hormones that regulate various bodily functions. When the thyroid gland does not produce the right amount of hormones, it leads to thyroid disease.

Hyperthyroidism occurs when the thyroid gland produces too much hormone, causing the body to use too much energy. This can result in feeling tired or having a faster than usual heart rate. Additionally, it can cause weight loss and nervousness.

On the other hand, hypothyroidism occurs when the thyroid gland does not produce enough hormone. This can make you feel tired, gain weight, and become intolerant to cold temperatures.

Thyroiditis is an inflammation of the thyroid gland, which can cause hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism. It can also cause pain and swelling in the neck.

Hashimoto’s thyroiditis is an autoimmune disorder where the body attacks the thyroid gland, leading to hypothyroidism. Symptoms include weight gain, fatigue, and sensitivity to cold temperatures.

## Etiology of Thyroid Disease

The two main kinds of thyroid disease include hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism.Both may be caused by diseases that affect how the thyroid functions.

Conditions that result to hypothyroidism are:

- Thyroiditis – there is inflammation of the gland and can lower the amount of hormone produced.
- Hashimoto’s thyroiditis – this is a painless but autoimmune condition where the cells of the body attack and damage the thyroid. The disease is inherited.
- Postpartum thyroiditis – this happens in 5% to 9% of women after childbirth and is usually a temporary condition.
- Iodine deficiency – lack of iodine results in low thyroid hormones produced.
- Non-functioning thyroid – the gland fails to function at birth and affects 1 in 4000 newborns.

Conditions that result in hyperthyroidism are:

- Grave’s Disease – an overactive thyroid that produces too much hormones.This is known as diffuse toxic goiter.
- Nodules – hyperthyroidism may be caused by nodules that are overactive within the thyroid.A single nodule is called toxic autonomously functioning thyroid nodule, while a gland with several nodules is called a toxic multi-nodular goiter.
- Thyroiditis – it can either be painful or not felt at all. In this condition the thyroid releases hormones that were stored and may last for a few weeks or months.
- Excessive Iodine –Too much iodine intake can make the thyroid produce more hormones than necessary.This may be found in some drugs like amiodarone,a heart medication and some cough syrups.
