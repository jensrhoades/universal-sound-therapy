## Short Description of Kidney and Liver Issues

Kidney issues can affect the body's ability to clean the blood, filter extra water out of the blood and maintain correct blood pressure. It also has an effect on red blood cell production and vitamin D metabolism necessary for optimum bone health.

Liver issues can be inherited or may be due to several factors that cause damage to the organ such as alcohol, viruses and obesity.

## About Kidney & Liver Issues

### Kidney Issues

When your body is affected with kidney issues, waste products and fluid may not be filtered out and build up. Healthy kidneys function to: keep a balance of water and minerals in your blood, remove waste from your blood, make renin to help manage blood pressure, make erythropoietin to prompt red blood cell production, and make an active form of vitamin D needed for bone health.

### Liver Issues

The liver is the second biggest organ in the body just after the skin. It separates nutrients and waste as they move through the digestive system. It is also responsible for the production of bile. Liver issues refer to any of several conditions that affect and damage your liver. Over time, liver issues can result to cirrhosis or scarring.

## Etiology of Kidney & Liver Issues

### Acute Kidney Issues

Primary causes include: lack of blood flow to the kidneys, direct damage to the kidneys, and backed up urine in the kidneys.

Chronic Kidney Issues – When the kidneys poorly function or fail to work for more than three months. High blood pressure and diabetes are the two most common causes.

## Causes of Liver Issues

1. Hepatitis A – People get infected by eating or drinking food or water contaminated with fecal material.
2. Hepatitis B – Patients can be infected through unprotected sex, using illegal drugs via sharing of needles.
3. Hepatitis C – You can get infected from tainted blood that is transfused to you.
4. Autoimmune hepatitis – results to inflammation of the liver and can lead to other disorders and even liver failure.
5. Primary biliary cholangitis – the disease attacks the tiny tubes in the liver called bile ducts.
6. Primary sclerosing cholangitis – results to scarring of the bile ducts and eventually blocks them.
