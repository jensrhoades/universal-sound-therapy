# How Sound Therapy Can Help Manage Tinnitus Symptoms

Are the ringing in your ears driving you crazy? [Tinnitus](https://universalsoundtherapy.com/product/tinnitus/), or the perception of sound that has no external source, affects countless people around the world, leading to a significant decrease in quality of life. Here, we'll explore how sound therapy can help manage tinnitus symptoms, as well as how to go about setting up a sound therapy regimen. ### Quick Recap

Sound therapy is a form of treatment that uses sound to reduce the symptoms of tinnitus. One type of sound therapy involves wearing earphones and listening to soothing, customized sounds such as gentle music or environmental noises while another involves playing frequencies to the body as a tool in which the body can use those tools and repair damaged or changed frequencies.

## What is Sound Therapy for Tinnitus?

Sound therapy is a form of therapy used to treat tinnitus, a condition where an individual experiences ringing or buzzing in the ears due to sound-processing difficulties in their auditory system. Sound therapy is based on the idea that exposing individuals with tinnitus to soothing, calming sounds or specific predetermined frequencies will reduce their tinnitus symptoms and help them manage related distress. The argument for using sound therapy as a treatment for tinnitus hinges on the belief that by interrupting loud noises or repairing damaged frequencies, the body will retrain itself to recognize tinnitus sounds as more natural and less disruptive or in the best scenario, get rid of the tinnitus sounds completely. In addition, proponents of sound therapy suggest that it can help people with tinnitus increase their awareness of silence and help counteract feelings of anxiety and depression often associated with the condition.

However, not all studies agree on how effective sound therapy is as a treatment for tinnitus symptoms. Skeptics point out that there isn't strong evidence to support the effectiveness of sound therapy beyond anecdotal reports. Furthermore, some experts contend that if sound therapy does provide relief from tinnitus symptoms, the effects may only be temporary.

Regardless of which side of the debate you may find yourself on, one thing remains clear: sound therapy holds potential for managing tinnitus symptoms and does have low risk factors as a treatment option. With that in mind, let's take a look at some of the different types of sound therapies available to those looking to better manage their tinnitus symptoms.

## Types of Sound Therapy

The most common approaches to sound therapy include: cymatic sound therapy, auditory modifications, white noise machines, tinnitus retraining therapy (TRT), hypnosis, cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), and deep brain stimulation (DBS). Auditory modifications involve introducing calming or distracting sounds into the person's environment in order to reduce the perceived intensity of their symptoms. Cymatic therapy relies on reintroduction of healthy frequencies to replace frequencies that have changed because of injury or illness.  White noise machines emit random signals which interfere with the tinnitus while simultaneously providing a more pleasant distraction. Tinnitus Retraining Therapy is similar to auditory modifications but adds in counseling components to promote emotional adjustment and coping skills such as relaxation techniques. Hypnosis utilizes breathing and visualization techniques to help individuals relax and control their reactions to their tinnitus symptoms. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy uses therapies like relaxation exercises or lifestyle changes to reduce tinnitus-related stress levels. Finally, Deep Brain Stimulation is an experimental surgical procedure utilizing electrodes embedded directly into the brain tissue to manipulate nerve activity in order to lessen tinnitus symptoms.

Each type of sound therapy has its own advantages and disadvantages before beginning any approach it is important for individuals suffering from tinnitus symptoms to first speak with an audiologist or medical professional about their condition in order to identify an appropriate treatment approach for their unique situation. The ultimate success of sound therapy in managing tinnitus depends upon selecting a program that works best for each individual case. ### Cymatic Sound Therapy

Cymatic Sound Therapy is based on the work of Dr. Peter  Guy Manners, a British Osteopath that started working with sound as a healing tool back in the 1960's.  His work showed that a persons natural healthy frequencies can be altered by illness or injury, and that reintroducing the correct frequencies back to the body, allows the body to correct itself and return to a healthy state.

Proponents of Cymatics believe this to be a very safe option when treating tinnitus and have seen very positive results.

## Masking

Masking is another approach to relieving tinnitus symptoms. It involves using external sound sources to distract from or mask the presence of tinnitus. Masking can help reduce temporary tinnitus symptoms caused by acute loud noise, or it can provide some relief for those with chronic tinnitus.

Proponents of masking believe that it is a safe, effective way to reduce the perception of tinnitus in both the short- and long-term. Additionally, it may be an effective way to reduce anxiety levels associated with the condition.

Opponents of masking argue that it does not effectively address the root cause of tinnitus, nor does it provide significant long-term relief. Further, they suggest that overusing this approach may lead to increased dependence on external sounds, which could eventually make it harder to ignore the tinnitus once there is no longer a sound source present.

Given these conflicting views, it appears more research is needed to better understand the impact of masking on long-term tinnitus symptom management. Regardless, many people have found some degree of alleviation through this approach and should consider its potential benefits.

Ultimately, while masking may offer short-term relief for some individuals with tinnitus, other solutions may be necessary for lasting results. The next section will explore another potential method for managing tinnitus symptoms: habituation.

### Habituation

Habituation is an important treatment for tinnitus symptoms. It is a process of reducing the emotional reactivity to the symptom itself. By engaging in habituation, tinnitus sufferers can gradually adapt to their condition and eventually diminish their emotional reaction to the sound, making it less intrusive and disruptive in their lives.

This process of adapting and learning to cope with minor disruptions has been scientifically proven as an effective approach among individuals who struggle with different types of chronic pain. The same applies to those managing tinnitus. Those who practice habituation often report feeling more in control of the condition and being able to better cope with its presence in their life.

On the other hand, there are some people who believe that habituating to tinnitus implies minimizing or ignoring it completely; consequently, they may be putting themselves at risk for further damage by not fully addressing their condition. Therefore it is important to consult a physician or audiologists when seeking treatment for tinnitus.

No matter what side of this argument you're on, habituation is a potential way to help manage tinnitus symptoms and make them less disruptive in your life.

## How to Use Sound Therapy to Manage Tinnitus

Sound therapy is an effective method for managing tinnitus. It helps reduce its effects over time, helping to keep it under control and improve the quality of life of those affected. There are three main methods of sound therapy: Cymatics, masking and retraining.  Cymatics uses replacing damaged frequencies with new healthy ones, masking uses a noise that is louder than the tinnitus to cover up the bothersome sound, while retraining works on reconditioning the brain to lessen the impact of tinnitus.

Cymatics has been shown to be very successful in both short term and long term gains on dealing with tinnitus.  It works by playing frequencies provided via headphones.  Interestingly, the headphones do not have to be placed on the ears as actually hearing the frequencies is not necessary.  Frequencies just need to be introduced to the body for them to have an effect.  This is a program that has shown to be rather inexpensive but does take a commitment of time.

Masking is often used as a short-term solution for people who find their tinnitus too distracting or uncomfortable to bear. It works by using noises at a level that is louder than the tinnitus, so that it meets or exceeds the volume of the unwanted sound and thereby masks it in some way. This can be done through headphones with prerecorded soundtracks, or by using specialized devices called 'tinnitus maskers' which produce white noise in a similar range. Regardless of the actual source of noise, masking can generally be successful in reducing the discomfort associated with tinnitus and giving users more peace and quiet when needed.

Retraining works in a different but complementary way by gradually teaching you to ignore your tinnitus sounds, rather than trying to block them out entirely. This involves working with a qualified therapist to modify your awareness of your tinnitus and repairing how your nervous system responds to it. The goal is to retrain your brain so it no longer pays attention to these bothersome background noises, ultimately leading to lower overall levels of stress and improved quality of life despite living with this condition.

Sound therapy is an effective method for managing tinnitus symptoms whether you choose masking or retraining techniques, but there are both pros and cons to each approach. Masking offers immediate relief from intense tinnitus symptoms, but since it does not address any underlying causes or attempt to reprogram how you react to sounds like your own tinnitus, long term results tend to be less successful than retraining approaches. Retraining does require more effort up front but its long-term benefits make it worth investing in for many people living with chronic tinnitus.

## Risks of Sound Therapy

Sound therapy has been found to be a very successful treatment option for those suffering from tinnitus symptoms, however, with any sort of treatment there are potential risks associated. One such risk is the possibility of treating tinnitus without understanding the underlying causes. Although it's possible to manage tinnitus symptoms through sound therapy, ignoring the cause of the condition can lead to future problems.

Another risk associated with sound therapy is that it may not provide the desired results. Not everyone experiences the same level of relief when using sound therapy and some individuals may find that it doesn't help their symptoms at all. Additionally, some lifestyles may not be conducive to long-term use of sound therapy as a solution. It's important to discuss this with your health care professional before committing to sound therapy as a treatment solution.

On the flip side, there are benefits that can also come with regular use of sound therapy. It can aid in relaxation and reduction of stress levels, creating an overall healthier lifestyle.

Therefore, there are both pros and cons when considering generally safe treatments for tinnitus such as sound therapy. Ultimately, each individual needs to determine if the benefits of using sound therapy outweigh the potential risks when making a decision regarding treatment options.
