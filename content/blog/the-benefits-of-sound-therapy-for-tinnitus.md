## What is Sound Therapy for Tinnitus?

Sound therapy for tinnitus is an evidence-based approach to managing the symptoms of this chronic condition. It involves using sound to habituate or decrease the audibility of the internal phantom noise experienced by those with tinnitus. While research suggests sound therapy can be effective in reducing symptoms, opinions on its use vary among experts.

On one hand, some experts argue that sound therapy has merit because it focuses on habituating a patient's brain and central auditory pathways to the sensation of the phantom noise associated with tinnitus. Masking Sound Therapy helps people become accustomed to their tinnitus and experience less annoyance from their tinnitus over time. Cymatic Sound Therapy provides the correct frequencies to the body so it can repair or replace damaged frequencies that are the result of illness or injury. For these reasons, many individuals have reported a great deal of relief from sound therapy treatments.

On the other hand, some experts caution that although sound therapy may reduce symptoms temporarily, long-term effects have yet to be determined. In addition, there are instances where sound therapy failed or was not effective in controlling the annoying phantom noises related to tinnitus. At present, more research is needed to understand why some individuals benefit from sound therapies while others do not.

Regardless of debate regarding its efficacy in providing lasting relief, sound therapy remains a common and potentially helpful tool for managing tinnitus symptoms. Moving forward in exploring the potential benefits of sound therapy, let's take a closer look at the mental benefits it may bring to those who suffer from this chronic condition.

- A 2014 systematic review found that sound therapy (including tailored music, white noise and other sounds) can have a large effect on improving tinnitus symptoms in a significant number of patients.
