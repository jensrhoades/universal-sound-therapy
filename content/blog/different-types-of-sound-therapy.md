## Different Types of Sound Therapy

Sound therapy, also referred to as music therapy, is an evidence-based practice that uses sound and music to achieve a desired physical, emotional or cognitive result. There are numerous forms of sound therapy that practitioners use in various ways.

He developed a series of frequencies designed to work on all the different parts of the body,  He found that frequencies in our body change when injured or when ill and by providing the proper frequency back to the body, it will work to change the altered frequency and return to its natural healthy state.

Another type of sound therapy is vocal or talking therapies. This form of sound therapy incorporates the human voice to help a client express emotion, reduce stress and heal pain. The therapist may encourage the client to speak to create a better understanding of their thoughts and feelings. The sound produced by talking provides relaxation and comfort for the client, which helps them to become more aware of the underlying cause of their distress.

Another type of sound therapy is musical improvisation. This style of therapy involves spontaneously creating musical sounds using instruments or voice. This form of music making allows the client to express themselves without any limitations, while developing self-expression and artistry. It gives clients an opportunity to experience pleasure as they explore different sounds and collaborate with others in music-making. Both therapists and clients have reported various physical and psychological benefits from this approach such as improved mood and insight into their emotions.

### Relaxation Therapy

Relaxation therapy is the use of sound to reduce physical and mental stress by calming and focusing the mind. It typically involves listening to soothing music, or other natural sounds such as ocean waves or chirping birds. When used in combination with breathing exercises and guided visualization, relaxation therapy can help lower heart rate, reduce blood pressure, improve sleep quality, and increase overall feelings of well-being. On the flip side however, some research suggests that not all music and natural sounds are equally effective when it comes to achieving relaxation.

While there have yet to be any studies that directly link specific types of music to the relaxation response, there is consensus within the field that classical music has a better chance of producing desired results than other kinds of more modern or electronically produced music. This means that if one's goal is to achieve a sense of calmness in an environment where they are surrounded by less desirable sounds (traffic noise, loud appliances etc.), they may be best advised to stick with traditional instrumental pieces versus others options – if available.

The effectiveness of relaxation therapy is also disputed depending upon the individual's level of stress at the time of treatment. Some individuals claim that listening to soothing sound for no more than 15 minutes daily significantly reduces their stress levels. Others report only short-term benefits or no effect at all after trying this type of therapy for several weeks.

Overall, relaxing with sound does offer potential therapeutic benefits for those who find themselves feeling overwhelmed in certain life situations. Its ability to lower physical and emotional tension when used correctly can lead to improved performance in both work and personal environments.
